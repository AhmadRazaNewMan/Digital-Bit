# Rolling Deploys and Failure Budgets
## What
Rolling deploys and failure budgets are two crucial concepts in DevOps that help ensure the reliability and availability of software systems. Rolling deploys refer to the practice of incrementally updating a system by replacing small portions of it with new versions, while minimizing downtime and reducing the risk of errors. Failure budgets, on the other hand, represent the acceptable amount of failure or error that a system can tolerate before corrective action is taken.

## Why
The importance of rolling deploys and failure budgets lies in their ability to balance the need for continuous improvement and innovation with the need for reliability and stability. By implementing rolling deploys, teams can quickly recover from errors and minimize the impact on users. Failure budgets help teams to prioritize and manage risk, ensuring that the system remains operational even in the event of failures.

## How
To implement rolling deploys and failure budgets, teams can follow a few best practices:
* Monitor system performance and set clear failure budgets
* Implement automated testing and validation
* Use canary releases or blue-green deployments to test new versions
* Continuously monitor and analyze system logs and metrics
* Establish clear communication channels and incident response plans

## One exercise or command
Try running a canary release using Kubernetes: `kubectl rollout update deployment <deployment-name> --image=<new-image-name>` and then verify the rollout status with `kubectl rollout status deployment <deployment-name>`.
